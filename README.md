# Computational illustrations for Digital Twins for Food Processing

Public repository: https://github.com/ketney1982/digital-twins-food-processing-reproducibility. The release tagged `manuscript-2026-10-31` is the state that accompanies the delivered text of the monograph; paths of the form `reproducibility/...` in the book refer to the root of this repository. Licence: MIT (see `LICENSE`); the files in `data/external` are readings or transcriptions of published data and remain subject to the terms of their sources (the apple-drying curves come from a CC BY paper).

This companion package supports the consolidated methodological monograph. It contains limiting-equation verification, reduced simulations, constructed response curves, assigned diagnostic/observer quantities and conditional accounting scenarios. It contains no industrial validation dataset or independently qualified food-process controller.

## Reproduction

Use an environment satisfying requirements.txt and run `python run_all_benchmarks.py`. The runner regenerates the 23 result CSVs (22 earlier ones plus the Chapter 9 closed-loop controller comparison described below), performs numerical regression and analytical checks, and regenerates the 14 figures. Regression agreement checks implementation consistency; it does not establish physical validity. The inherited evidence register supplies the existing parameter and computational metadata. Historical review entries in it are not new validation results.

## Explicit schema and label corrections

The old `auroc` column is now `constructed_diagnostic_score`. The value 0.996 is algebraically assigned; no continuous score–label pairs or empirical ROC curve are supplied. No detector was run on generated or industrial telemetry: precision, recall, false-alarm rate, latency and residual bias of `Ch03_Data_Readiness_Gate_ROC_Benchmark.csv` are assigned inputs, and the dark-blue trace of Figure 3.1(a), labelled as the gate-reconstructed stream in earlier versions of the figure, is a low-noise copy of the imposed signal, not the output of an executed gate (see below).

The old `mean_cpu_ms_per_step` and `wcet_99_9pct_ms` columns are now `assigned_mean_time_ms` and `assigned_tail_time_ms`. These remain assigned quantities, with no timing experiment or WCET proof. Fields containing 95CI are historical names for assigned ranges.

Chapter 4's polynomial, oracle-perturbed, oracle-residual and assigned-factor curves are labelled by their construction. The separate prescribed ablation slots are labelled assigned error slots. None establishes an independently trained MLP, LSTM, GPR or UDE result. The Chapter 12 recurrent comparator is an assigned design slot, not a trained LSTM. Historical CSV filenames remain stable to preserve manuscript cross-references.

Only textual schema labels and identifiers changed in the archived result and regression-reference CSVs; their numerical values were preserved. The generator, reader and comparison code use the same names. Figures retain the assigned/reduced values and are interpreted through their captions; panel (a) of Figure 10.1 also shows the resolved-model values (see below).

## Scope of the available evidence

The scalar diffusion verification exercises the specified fixed-domain dimensionless problem. It does not verify coupled multiphase transport, moving geometry or a food constitutive law; Table 2.7 of Chapter 2 records, for each model family of that chapter, the verification available in the book. Parameters have declared design, derivation or reference origins; this is distinct from calibration for an actual matrix. The application comparisons use prescribed response factors and other reduced constructions. Their rankings, error bars and event counts are not independently measured industrial performance.

No online SHAP pipeline, target-hardware timing trial, HiL qualification, microbial challenge study, controller comparison on a plant or validated plant model, or plant safety certificate is established; the only executed controller comparison is the reduced-model benchmark of Chapter 9 (below). Carbon and economic figures remain conditional consequences of assigned inputs. The revised FEFO text also corrects the Pallet 19 routing example: its declared lower quality-life quantile is non-positive, so the proposed 16-hour route is inadmissible under that criterion.

## Practical use

Use the book's new practice sections to define a limited engineering decision, causal information, parameter origin, uncertainty interpretation, recommendation expiry and a hold/refusal option. Appendices E–H specify independent comparisons, process assurance, parameter admissibility and the claim–evidence–decision relation. Proposed tests are not completed experiments. A reader may reproduce calculations and use the specifications for simulation, teaching and design review; operational authority requires separate application-specific evidence.

## Executed closed-loop controller comparison (Chapter 9, 31 October 2026)

`run_ch09_controller_comparison_benchmark()` in `run_all_benchmarks.py` (Section 9.7.8, Tables 9.6 and 9.7) simulates the heating section and holding tube of a continuous pasteuriser reduced to two lumped energy balances. Declared inputs are in `ch09_declared_design()`; the flow, the 72.0 °C critical limit and the lethality parameters are read from the Chapter 7 rows (`M4_Ch07_Safety`) of `data/kinetic_and_transport_parameters.csv`, and no parameter row was added. Six controllers are run: a fixed valve, PI with back-calculation anti-windup, a linear tracking MPC (exact QP, Lawson–Hanson least-distance programming), a nominal economic MPC with a hard critical limit (SciPy SLSQP), the same with a split-conformal back-off calibrated on 100 separate realisations, and the economic MPC followed by the sampled CBF-QP filter of Section 7.6.2. All use the same 200 disturbance realisations of 600 s (seeds 9090000–9090199; calibration seeds 9190000–9190099), the same 2 s sampling, valve limits, sensor noise and measured signals. Constraints are evaluated on the true temperatures every 0.5 s. Output: `results/Ch09_Closed_Loop_Controller_Comparison_Benchmark.csv`, which gives an estimate, 95% interval, median and 5th/95th percentiles per controller and metric (bootstrap over realisations for means, Clopper–Pearson for proportions), with an identical regression reference in `benchmarks/`. The rows `computation_time_per_step_*` are wall-clock times measured on the machine that runs the script and are excluded from the numerical check. The other rows are compared with a relative tolerance of 1e-6 because the closed loops contain an iterative solver. Rerunning three realisations reproduced the stored per-run values exactly. The `evidence_status` column keeps the package-wide default label; the column `evidence_label` states the precise status: executed benchmark on a reduced model, not validation. The stored file was produced with Python 3.11.16, NumPy 2.4.6 and SciPy 1.17.1 (not the versions pinned in `requirements.txt`). The run took 42 min on a desktop PC (Intel64 Family 6 Model 165) that was simultaneously running other jobs at full processor load, so the stored computation times include pre-emption delays. `python -c "import run_all_benchmarks as r; r.run_ch09_controller_comparison_benchmark(r.load_parameters())"` runs this part alone.

## Additional verification (reduction error, Chapter 10)

`verification/verify_ch10_reduction_error.py` compares the three-node retort model of Scenario IA with a resolved axisymmetric finite-volume conduction solution (solver checked against the analytical series and by grid refinement) and evaluates every retort policy of Table 10.3 with that resolved model over the same 500 Monte Carlo trials: the two constant-retort-temperature schedules, the assigned variable-retort-temperature schedule, the archived adaptive branch (replayed with its privileged information, with the archived 22% dip attenuation and with the full dip) and two realisable adaptive controllers that observe only the measured retort and initial temperatures (feed-forward) or, in addition, the centre temperature of one instrumented can (probe). Each policy is also tuned so that the empirical 1st percentile of resolved F0 reaches 8 min, with two-fold cross-fitting as an out-of-sample check. Measurement noise (SD 0.5 K, seed 2026), the instrumented can and the controllers are assigned. The resolved model is advanced mode by mode (the finite-volume operator is diagonalised; this reproduces the sparse solver to 1e-10 min in F0). Outputs are `Ch10_Reduction_Error_Solver_Checks.csv`, `Ch10_Reduction_Error_Trials.csv` (per policy and trial) and `Ch10_Reduction_Error_Summary.csv` (per policy: reduction error, F0 percentiles, under-target counts with one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson bounds, classification changes, retention, cook value, cycle time, margin and cross-fitted counts) in the same folder; they are not part of the 23 regression CSVs of `run_all_benchmarks.py`. The reduced-versus-resolved comparison is a model-to-model verification and the policy evaluation an executed benchmark on the resolved conduction model; neither is validation against heat-penetration data, and neither may be used to set a sterilisation time.

`verification/benchmark_ch04_drying_architectures.py` runs the executed in-silico comparison of seven architectures (calibrated Fickian series, ridge polynomial, multilayer perceptron, Gaussian-process regression, parallel hybrid, UDE and LSTM) on a thin-slab drying generator, with whole-batch withholding, an out-of-domain set and a closure-shift set (Section 4.8.7). The `baseline` scenario (10 repeats) reproduces Table 4.6 and the earlier stored rows exactly. Since 31 October 2026 the same script also runs nine one-factor-at-a-time scenarios with 10 repeats each (Table 4.7): low and high training budget (perceptron epochs, convergence tolerance and restarts, LSTM epochs and seeds, UDE function evaluations, Gaussian-process optimiser restarts), 10, 20 and 80 training batches, noise standard deviation 0.01 and 0.06 kg/kg, training on the power-law closure with the exponential closure as the shift set, and a finite surface mass-transfer coefficient of 1e-7 m/s (assumed). `Ch04_Executed_Drying_Comparison_Repeats.csv` gains the scenario and setting columns, `rank_in_repeat` and `mlp_epochs_mean`; `Ch04_Executed_Drying_Comparison_Summary.csv` gains, per scenario, condition and model, a bootstrap 95% interval of the mean RMSE over repeats (2000 resamples, seed 12345), the mean, minimum and maximum rank within repeats, the share of repeats in which the model was best and Kendall's tau of the mean-RMSE order against the baseline. The condition label `CLOSURE_B` is now `CLOSURE_SHIFT`, because the shift set uses closure A when training is on closure B. Options: `--scenarios`, `--workers`, `--repeats` and `--cache` (JSON-lines file of finished repeats, for resuming). It needs scikit-learn and PyTorch (CPU) in addition to requirements.txt. Results hold for the declared generators only; they are not physical validation and are not part of the regression CSVs of `run_all_benchmarks.py`.

`verification/verify_ch10_ch11_ch12_reduction_error.py` checks three further reductions against resolved counterparts built from declared equations and parameters: the Chapter 10 UHT campaign (an assigned scenario, so only an accounting check: event-driven campaign instead of integer-count accounting, plus a sensitivity sweep over an assigned thermal-feedback gain; no resolved exchanger model exists), the Chapter 11 drying response (one-dimensional Fickian slab with the declared diffusivity and GAB equilibrium, half-thickness calibrated on the nominal case) and the Chapter 12 fed-batch (time-step refinement). It writes `Ch10_UHT_Reduction_Check.csv`, `Ch11_Drying_Reduction_Check.csv` and `Ch12_Fermentation_Reduction_Check.csv`. These are model-to-model checks, not validation; baking, extrusion and the cold chain are not covered.

`verification/extract_apple_drying_data.py` digitises the moisture-ratio curves of a CC BY paper (Processes 2026, 14(12), 2015, https://doi.org/10.3390/pr14122015) from the vector figures of its PDF into `data/external/apple_drying_curves_Processes2026.csv`; the digitised curves reproduce the paper's ten published log-linear slopes within 2.04% (`Ch04_Physical_Drying_Digitisation_Check.csv`). `verification/benchmark_ch04_physical_drying.py` compares eight architectures on these ten curves with whole curves withheld (replicate transfer, temperature extrapolation and interpolation) and writes `Ch04_Physical_Drying_Folds.csv` and `Ch04_Physical_Drying_Summary.csv` (Table 4.8, formerly 4.7). Since 31 October 2026 it also repeats the stochastic learners (M3, M6, M7) with four further training seeds (100, 200, 300, 400); the `variant` column distinguishes `nominal_seed0` (Table 4.8, identical to the earlier stored rows) from `nominal_seed1` to `nominal_seed4`, models that are not refitted are copied from `nominal_seed0` (`refitted_in_variant`), and the summary gives the rank of each model within a variant and split group. `--budget-variants` adds low and high training budgets; these were not run for the stored files. The data come from one laboratory and one product and are not an industrial validation. On Windows, `import torch` must precede numpy, scipy and scikit-learn.

`verification/verify_ch11_ch12_baking_extrusion_coldchain.py` checks the extrusion exponents against the declared power-law rheology, reports the surface-temperature sensitivity implied by the baking acrylamide exponent (no resolved baking model can be built from the declared parameters) and tests the cold-chain scenario against a fluctuating core temperature and against the declared Listeria growth parameters; it writes `Ch11_Extrusion_Reduction_Check.csv`, `Ch11_Baking_Reduction_Check.csv` and `Ch12_ColdChain_Reduction_Check.csv`.

`verification/verify_ch11_baking_resolved.py` runs a conduction-only model of the 42 mm half-thickness bread loaf under two supplied oven programmes (artisan: 240 °C for 15 min, then 210 °C; wholemeal: 165 °C) with assumed typical properties and three heat-transfer coefficients, and writes `Ch11_Baking_Resolved_Check.csv`: time for the core to reach the declared 96 °C gelatinisation threshold (110–203 min, against the 18.2 min used by the reduced model) and the acrylamide exponent on the heat-transfer coefficient (3.6–4.4 where a dry crust forms, against the assigned 1.35). The model has no vapour transport, so its heating times are a conditional result of this model, not a proven bound on baking time and not a validation.

## Published laboratory data used for Chapter 11 (30 October 2026)

`verification/verify_ch11_baking_physical.py` uses `data/external/bread_baking_temperatures_Zanoni1993.csv`: oven, crust and crumb temperatures of a bread baked at 203 °C for 30 min (Zanoni, Peri & Pierucci, J. Food Eng. 19 (1993) 389-398, Fig. 1). The paper gives the curves only as a raster figure; the values at 5 min intervals were read from an enlarged rendering (uncertainty about ±3 °C) and are not a numerical table of the paper. With the gelatinisation kinetics of Purlis (J. Food Eng. 103 (2011) 92-102) the coldest point reaches a degree of 0.98 at 26.5 min; a fitted one-dimensional model with effective conductivity is transferred to the scenario loaf and shows that the assigned 18.2 min is consistent with a half-thickness of about 19-23 mm (21-26 mm for 22 min), not the 42 mm of the original scenario. The same script refits the model one factor at a time (read temperatures -3 and +3 °C, evaporable water fraction 0.15 and 0.35, specific heat 2600 and 3400 J/(kg K); effective conductivity 0.59-0.77 W/(m K), heat-transfer coefficients 46-58 W/(m² K) at the top and 32-40 W/(m² K) at the bottom) and computes the redefined bread of Scenario IIA-2, which is the measured loaf (dough height 46 mm, measured expansion law over its 30 min range) on the artisan programme with a residence of 30 min: the coldest point reaches a gelatinisation degree of 0.98 after 24-28 min, and over the 250 heat-transfer multipliers of `data/monte_carlo_disturbances_seed42.csv` no trial is below 0.98 at 30 min (99.99 ± 0.01 % nominal, 99.78 ± 0.22 % for the slowest refitted set); with 25 or 12 W/(m² K) the criterion needs 32 or 43 min. This is an executed benchmark calibrated on one loaf; the programme transfer is an extrapolation. Output: `Ch11_Baking_Physical_Check.csv`.

`verification/verify_ch11_ch12_literature_remaining_items.py` compares the remaining quantities of Chapters 11 and 12 with published data: extruder transit times (Kumar et al., J.
